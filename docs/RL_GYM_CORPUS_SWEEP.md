**A negative margin does not bring branch 7 closer.** `record_remaining_assignments` runs
until every vehicle finishes its route and only then gives up, at `end_time + 14,400`
(`FleetSimulationBase.py` ~625, written `2 * 7200`), where it **breaks the loop with vehicles
still en route**. That break is the only way a rider who accepted an offer can end the day
without a pickup, so **the distance between the longest tail and the cap is the indicator**,
and the margin is not. Negative margins are simply **expected on busier days**: more
requests arriving late means more trips still running at `end_time`, which the tail then
completes. Decisions on `w_horizon` stay with Ritun (§6.4, P2.13).

**The demand loader applies two filters, in this order** (`demand.py` ~103-105): `file` is
rows in the CSV; `window` keeps `rq_time` in `[start_time, end_time)`; `o=d drop` is how many of
those are then removed because their `start` node equals their `end` node; `loaded` is what
reaches the simulation. A dropped row never enters `future_requests`, never reaches
`build_assignment_context` and never calls `record_user`, so it is correctly in no bucket —
which is why it must not be counted as loaded. The `o=d` column, by contrast, counts
requests the **operator** resolved as origin-equals-destination, and is 0 throughout.

`margin` is `end_time − last_dropoff`; `tail s` is the simulated seconds `record_remaining_assignments`
added past `end_time`; `in flt` is the riders picked up but not yet dropped off when that
tail starts; `unsrv` is §6.4's branch 7. Asserted per day by the sweep:
`steps + empty + resv + o=d + dup rid = loaded`, `exits = loaded`, and the eight §6.4
terminal outcomes summing to `loaded`.
